### Sample size sufficiency

n=9,791 with an overall win rate ~11-13% → roughly 1,100-1,300 positive-class events feeding a
3-parameter logistic fit (intercept + 2 slopes). Well above the ~10 events/parameter rule of thumb
for stable MLE logistic regression. `barrel_pct` and `hard_hit_pct` are weakly correlated with each
other (r=0.046 within v4.5, r=0.077 pooled) — collinearity is not a concern. **No regularization
applied** — plain MLE (Newton/BFGS) with standard errors from the observed Fisher information
(`(Xᵀ diag(p(1-p)) X)⁻¹`). Regularization (ridge/L2) would be warranted with fewer events per
parameter or meaningful collinearity; neither applies here, so an unregularized fit is the right
call and avoids introducing an extra, undocumented shrinkage assumption on top of what's already
being asked for.

### Fit 2 — offset-controlled (methodological addition — flagged deviation)

**This is not what was literally asked for; flagging it as a deliberate addition because I think
Fit 1 alone is the wrong quantity to bake into `sc_adj` as scoped, and the result changes the
picture enough that it needs to be shown before Phase 2 proceeds.**

`sc_adj` doesn't operate in isolation — it's one term added into a log-odds sum that already
includes season rate, hotness, splits, park, weather, and pitching (`mlb_hr_model.py`'s
`run_model()`). Fit 1 measures barrel%/hard-hit%'s **total** association with outcome, which
includes whatever part of that association is already explained by a batter's season HR rate (a
good hitter tends to have high season HR rate *and* high barrel% simultaneously — season rate is
itself downstream of the same batted-ball quality barrel%/hard-hit% measure). If Fit 1's
coefficients are baked directly into `sc_adj` as an *additional* additive log-odds term on top of
`sr`/`hot_adj`/etc., some of that credit is double-counted — the same class of risk already flagged
for Phase 4's `league_adj` design ("must stay fully independent of hot_adj/sc_adj to avoid
double-counting"). It turns out to apply here too, between `sc_adj` and the model's other factors,
not just for the new league-level component.

To isolate the **marginal** (incremental) contribution — the only quantity that's actually correct
to add on top of what the model already knows — I recomputed, per pick, the model's own game
probability with `sc_adj`'s contribution algebraically removed (`offset = logit(game_prob) −
log(sc_adj)` in log-odds space for v4.4/v4.5's additive-compounding formula; `game_prob / sc_adj`
in probability space for v4.2/v4.3's retired multiplicative-compounding formula — both recompound
through the same `1−(1−per_pa)^pp` step), then fit an **offset logistic regression**:
`logit(P(HR)) = offset + a + b1·log(barrel_ratio) + b2·log(hard_hit_ratio)`.

**Correctness check before trusting this (the result was surprising enough to verify):** round-trip
reconstruction — re-adding `log(sc_adj)` to the computed offset and recompounding — reproduces the
stored `model_prob` exactly on every spot-checked pick. Independently recomputing the same
"probability without `sc_adj`" from scratch using the raw stored factors (`season_rate`,
`split_adj`, `hot_adj`, `sp_adj`/`bp_adj`, `park_adj`, `temp_adj`, `wind_adj` — v4.5 picks only,
since only v4.5 stores raw `season_rate`) matches the inversion-based offset to within ~0.0002,
consistent with the 4-decimal rounding already baked into stored values. **No computational bug.**
Sample: n=9,243 (548 `per_pa_capped` picks excluded — inversion isn't valid when the per-PA rate was
clipped rather than computed directly; 780 more excluded for missing fields).

| Coefficient | Estimate | SE | 95% CI |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| intercept | −0.069 | 0.036 | [−0.138, +0.001] |
| **b1 (barrel)** | **−0.193** | 0.084 | **[−0.358, −0.029]** |
| **b2 (hard-hit)** | **+0.108** | 0.231 | **[−0.345, +0.561]** |

The intercept sitting near 0 (barely missing significance) is itself a good sign the offset is
well-specified — it means the model's other factors, on their own, are already close to correctly
calibrated for this subsample once Statcast is removed. But **the marginal `barrel_pct` coefficient
is negative and statistically significant; `hard_hit_pct`'s marginal coefficient is small and not
distinguishable from zero.** Per-version breakdown of this second fit is materially less stable
than Fit 1's:

| Version | n | b1 (barrel) | b2 (hard-hit) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| v4.2 | 810 | −0.333 ± 0.296 | +0.377 ± 0.800 |
| v4.3 | 4,283 | −0.307 ± 0.119 (sig.) | +0.530 ± 0.333 |
| v4.4 | 2,480 | −0.099 ± 0.180 | +0.115 ± 0.492 |
| v4.5 | 1,670 | +0.036 ± 0.164 | **−0.942 ± 0.439 (sig., opposite sign from v4.3)** |

Pooled univariate checks (one predictor at a time, same offset): barrel alone −0.167 [−0.291,
−0.044] (significant negative); hard-hit alone −0.227 [−0.563, +0.109] (not significant). These
don't fully agree with the joint fit's signs either (hard-hit flips from +0.108 joint to −0.227
univariate) — a sign of a genuinely weak, noisy residual signal once the dominant offset is
accounted for, not a stable, well-determined effect in either direction.

### What this means — flagging for your decision before Phase 2

**Two internally-consistent but substantively different answers to "what should the exponents be,"
and I don't think it's my call which one goes into `sc_adj`:**

1. **Fit 1 (raw/unconditional)** is stable across versions, matches what was literally asked for,
   and directly refines the rough diagnostic estimate. Taken at face value it says: lower the
   barrel exponent (0.40→~0.15), raise the hard-hit exponent (0.20→~1.15).
2. **Fit 2 (offset-controlled)** measures what `sc_adj` would actually be contributing *on top of*
   the rest of the model if plugged in as designed, and it's a materially different — and much
   less certain — story: no robustly-detectable positive marginal signal from either metric, an
   unstable/sign-flipping picture across versions, and a significant *negative* marginal
   coefficient on barrel% in two independent specifications (pooled joint fit, pooled univariate
   fit, and the v4.3 per-version fit).

I think Fit 2 is the statistically correct quantity given `sc_adj`'s role as an additive log-odds
term layered on top of factors that already partly explain the same variance — using Fit 1's
coefficients directly would reintroduce a version of the same double-counting problem the spec
explicitly wants Phase 4 to avoid. But Fit 2's own instability (sign flips between v4.3 and v4.5,
wide CIs, a non-intuitive significant-negative barrel% result) makes me reluctant to bake *those*
numbers in directly either without your sign-off — a negative barrel-percent exponent is a much
bigger behavioral change than a re-weighting, and I'd want to understand it isn't an artifact of
some subtler cross-version formula difference (e.g., `hot_adj`/`split_adj` shrinkage changed
between v4.2/v4.3 and v4.5, so "what the model already knows" isn't computed identically across the
pooled offset sample) before committing to it.

**This changes Phase 2's scope as written** — "replace the hardcoded exponents with the fitted
values from Phase 1" now has two candidate answers, not one, and the more statistically defensible
one (Fit 2) suggests `sc_adj` may deserve a much smaller role than a reweighting implies, possibly
bearing on whether it should remain a full independent multiplicative factor at all. Per your
standing instruction to stop and flag when a phase's findings would change a later phase's scope —
flagging this now rather than picking one and proceeding into Phase 2.

**How I'd suggest resolving it, for your call:**
- (a) Proceed with **Fit 1** as literally scoped — simplest, matches the original ask, known
  double-counting risk but bounded by the fact `sc_adj` is capped either way.
- (b) Proceed with **Fit 2** — more defensible given `sc_adj`'s additive role, but re-scope Phase 2
  to a much smaller/near-zero `sc_adj` contribution (or open the question of retiring it as a
  separate factor) rather than a simple exponent swap.
- (c) Investigate the v4.3-vs-v4.5 sign instability in Fit 2 further before deciding (e.g., restrict
  the offset-controlled fit to v4.5-only, where `hot_adj`/`sc_adj` shrinkage formulas match current
  production exactly, accepting the smaller n=1,670 sample) — a middle path that doesn't fully
  resolve the tension but removes the cross-version-formula-mismatch confound from consideration.

Not proceeding to Phase 2 until you weigh in.
