# v4.5 Root-Cause Follow-Up — 2026-08-11

Follow-up to `archive/diagnostics/v4.5_checkpoint_2026-08-11.md`. Diagnosis only — no changes to
`mlb_hr_model.py`, `check_results.py`, or any thresholds/constants. Same source data:
`results/picks.json`, 1,806 resolved (latest-window) picks, 58 Bet-tier, tracked
2026-08-02 through 2026-08-11.

**Bottom line up front:** the checkpoint's three questions converge on a single, sharp answer.
`sc_adj` sitting at its hard cap (1.15) is not just correlated with the failure — it is present in
**100% of the 43 repeat-player picks and 100% of the 7 cold-tagged picks** examined below. But §1
shows the cap itself isn't the whole story once you control for `model_prob`: the overprediction
also shows up in *uncapped* `sc_adj` picks that are merely in the high-probability zone for other
reasons. And §2 shows the underlying exponents were **never fit to outcome data at all** — they've
been hardcoded since the very first commit. This points at scoping v4.6 around **(a) lowering the
cap and re-deriving the exponents from outcome data together**, not either alone, **plus (b) a
player-level persistence/reappearance guard**, since `sc_adj` is season-cumulative and barely moves
day to day — once a player clears the cap, they stay capped for weeks regardless of current form.

## 1. Cap-population calibration

Split the **full tracked population** (1,806 resolved, not just Bet-tier) by whether `sc_adj` sits
at its hard cap (≥1.145, within 0.005 of 1.15):

| Group | n | Predicted avg | Actual rate | Diff |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `sc_adj` at cap | 532 | 19.32% | 14.10% | **−5.22pp** |
| `sc_adj` not at cap | 1,274 | 9.68% | 8.48% | −1.20pp |

At face value this looks like the cap is the whole story — the capped group is ~4.3x more
overpredicted. But the capped group also has a much higher average predicted probability (19.3% vs
9.7%), and we already know from the original checkpoint that overprediction concentrates in the
high-probability zone regardless of cause. So the real test is whether the effect survives
**controlling for zone** — restricting to `model_prob > 15%` (the same population from the original
checkpoint's §4):

| Group (model_prob > 15%) | n | Predicted avg | Actual rate | Diff |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `sc_adj` at cap | 372 | 23.15% | 14.52% | −8.63pp |
| `sc_adj` not at cap | 239 | 19.10% | 12.55% | −6.55pp |

The gap narrows a lot (−8.6pp vs −6.6pp, not −5.2pp vs −1.2pp) but doesn't close. A finer cut by
`sc_adj` band, same population:

| `sc_adj` band | n | Predicted avg | Actual rate | Diff |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| at cap (≥1.145) | 372 | 23.15% | 14.52% | −8.63pp |
| 1.05–1.145 | 138 | 19.38% | 13.04% | −6.33pp |
| 0.95–1.05 (neutral) | 73 | 19.32% | 12.33% | **−6.99pp** |
| 0.85–0.95 | 21 | 17.21% | 4.76% | −12.44pp (n=21, noisy) |
| <0.85 | 7 | 17.00% | 28.57% | +11.57pp (n=7, noisy) |

**Answer to the question as posed: both.** The at-cap group is somewhat worse than not-at-cap
(−8.6pp vs −6.6pp within the same probability zone), so lowering the cap would help at the margin.
But the **neutral `sc_adj` band (0.95–1.05, i.e. barely adjusted at all) still shows −7.0pp
overprediction** within the same `model_prob > 15%` population — nearly as bad as the capped group.
That group's high `model_prob` is being driven by other factors (`hot_adj`, `split_adj`, park, or
simply a genuinely high season rate), and it's *still* overpredicted almost as much. **This means
the cap is not the sole or even primary cause — something about being in the high-probability zone
itself is miscalibrated, independent of which factor put you there.** Lowering the `sc_adj` cap
alone would shave a few points off the worst subgroup but would not fix the −6 to −7pp floor that
persists even without `sc_adj` elevated at all. This is consistent with the original checkpoint's
§4 finding (near-zero correlation between any single factor's magnitude and calibration error) —
it isn't one factor's fault, it's a property of the zone.

## 2. Raw Statcast signal check

**Where did the 0.40/0.20 exponents come from?** Traced via `git log -p` across the full history of
`mlb_hr_model.py`: they are present, unchanged, in the very first commit (`787b6d8`, "Initial
commit") with no comment, derivation, or reference to any fitting exercise. Searched
`archive/VERSION_HISTORY.md` for any documented justification — none exists. The only "0.40" that
*was* fit to outcome data in this project's history is an unrelated number: `RECAL_B`'s dormant
logistic-recalibration slope from the v4.3 diagnostic (`archive/diagnostics/v4.3_diagnostic_2026-07-18.md`,
"slope of 0.40 (SE 0.05)" on `outcome ~ logit(model_prob)`) — that's a recalibration of the whole
model's output, not a fit of the Statcast exponents specifically, and it was never wired in
(`RECAL_A=0.0, RECAL_B=1.0`, pass-through). **Confirmed: the 0.40/0.20 exponents were assigned, not
fit, and have never been fit since.**

**Does the raw premise (higher barrel%/hard-hit% → more same-game HR) still hold?** Weakly, yes, but
it's a much smaller signal than the model's weighting assumes.

Point-biserial correlation with actual HR outcome, full resolved population:

| Metric | r | n |
|---|--:|--:|
| `barrel_pct` | +0.0247 | 1,718 |
| `hard_hit_pct` | +0.0652 | 1,731 |

Both are weakly positive (right direction), but very weak — for comparison, a well-calibrated
single predictor of a rare binary outcome like this would typically show a noticeably stronger
correlation than 0.02–0.07 if it were carrying as much weight as the model gives it. Decile tables
show a real but noisy gradient for both metrics (`hard_hit_pct` cleaner: decile 1 actual HR rate
7.5% → decile 10 14.4%, roughly monotonic; `barrel_pct` similar but flattens/dips in the top 2
deciles — deciles 9–10 both land at 12.2%, below decile 8's 19.2%).

**The more direct test — does barrel% still separate outcomes within the already-high-probability
zone that Bet-tier picks come from?** Restricting to `model_prob ≥ 20%` and splitting into barrel%
quartiles:

| Quartile | barrel% range | n | Predicted avg | Actual rate |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 | 2.9–10.9% | 77 | 23.9% | 13.0% |
| 2 | 11.0–12.3% | 78 | 25.6% | 14.1% |
| 3 | 12.4–14.4% | 77 | 24.7% | 15.6% |
| 4 | 14.4–24.4% | 78 | 27.6% | 14.1% |

Predicted probability rises steadily across quartiles (23.9% → 27.6%) as the model rewards higher
barrel%, but **actual outcome rate is flat** (13.0% → 14.1% → 15.6% → 14.1%, no real trend). Within
the zone that matters for staking, incremental barrel% is not buying the incremental confidence the
model assigns it.

**A rough empirical check on the exponents themselves** (univariate/joint logistic fits of outcome
on `log(barrel_pct/LG_BARREL)` and `log(hard_hit_pct/LG_HARD_HIT)`, unconditional on every other
model factor — this is *not* a clean partial-effect estimate, since good hitters are correlated on
every metric at once, but it's informative about magnitude given the exponents were never fit to
begin with):

| Predictor | Fitted slope (univariate) | Fitted slope (joint, both together) | Assumed exponent |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `log(barrel_pct/LG_BARREL)` | 0.333 | 0.282 | 0.40 |
| `log(hard_hit_pct/LG_HARD_HIT)` | 0.920 | 0.502 | **0.20** |

`barrel_pct`'s assumed exponent (0.40) is roughly in the right neighborhood of what a naive outcome
fit supports (0.28–0.33), maybe slightly high. `hard_hit_pct`'s assumed exponent (0.20) looks
**substantially too low** — the data-implied slope is 2.5–4.5x higher. Read cautiously (no
confounder control, small-sample rare-event logistic fit, correlated predictors), but combined with
the flat-actual-rate finding above, the more defensible read is: **the Statcast signal that does
exist in this data is weaker overall than 0.40+0.20 combined implies, and what signal there is looks
misallocated between the two components** — not "the premise is broken," but "it was never
calibrated and the current split doesn't match what the outcome data supports."

**Is there a common factor? Yes — one, unambiguously.**

| | n | `sc_adj` at cap | `hot_adj` at cap |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Repeat-player picks (13 players, 43 picks) | 43 | **43/43 (100.0%)** | 14/43 (32.6%) |
| Non-repeat picks (15 picks, 15 players) | 15 | 12/15 (80.0%) | — |

**Every single one of the 43 repeat-player picks has `sc_adj` pinned at exactly 1.150.** `hot_adj`,
by contrast, varies day to day as it should (32.6% capped, the rest moving with actual recent form —
JJ Bleday's `hot_adj` drifts 1.07→1.01 over the window, García's swings 1.14→1.17→1.00, consistent
with real week-to-week hotness fluctuation). **`sc_adj`-at-cap is the mechanism, not just a
correlate, of the player-repetition pattern.** The reason is structural, not statistical: `sc_adj`
is built from season-cumulative barrel%/hard-hit% (`stats=season`), which moves extremely slowly —
one game's batted-ball events barely nudge a 300+-BBE season total. Once a player's shrunk rate
clears 1.15, they will keep clearing it for weeks regardless of what happens in any single game.
Combined with a favorable park/pitcher matchup on a given day (which *does* vary), that's enough to
push the same 13 players back over the Bet threshold again and again — the model isn't drawing from
a fresh pool of "hot right now" candidates each day, it's mostly cycling through a small, nearly
static set of players who cleared the Statcast cap once and stay flagged.

**`sc_adj` is at its 1.150 cap in all 7 of 7 (100%).** Dominant positive log-odds factor per pick:

player whose `hot_adj` has been pinned at the "ice cold" floor on three separate dates, yet who
keeps re-clearing the Bet threshold because `sc_adj`'s season-cumulative rate hasn't moved and stays
capped regardless. The other 4 cold-tagged picks are one-off appearances but show the identical
signature (`sc_adj` capped, `hot_adj` at or near floor).

## Scoping implications for v4.6 (diagnosis only — no changes made)

Putting §1–§4 together:

- **§1 rules out "just lower the cap" as a complete fix** — the overprediction persists (−7pp) even
  in the `sc_adj`-neutral band within the high-probability zone. Something about compounding several
  moderately-positive factors together is miscalibrated independent of any single factor's extremity.
- **§2 gives the clearest actionable lead:** the 0.40/0.20 exponents were never fit to outcome data
  and a rough outcome-based check suggests `hard_hit_pct` is underweighted relative to `barrel_pct`
  by a factor of roughly 2–4x, and that the combined Statcast signal in this sample is weaker than
  either exponent implies once you're already in the high-probability zone. Re-deriving both
  exponents from outcome data (a real fit, unlike the current constants) is a concrete, well-defined
  v4.6 candidate — separate from adjusting the cap.
- **§3 and §4 together identify a second, structurally distinct problem:** `sc_adj`'s season-
  cumulative construction makes it nearly static per player for weeks at a time, so once a player
  clears the cap they stay in the Bet-eligible pool regardless of current form — including, per §4,
  overriding an explicit "cooling off" (`hot_adj` floor) signal entirely. This is not a
  weighting/exponent problem and wouldn't be fixed by re-deriving 0.40/0.20 alone; it needs either a
  faster-moving Statcast window (e.g. a recency-weighted or rolling BBE sample instead of full-season
  cumulative) or an explicit interaction rule (e.g. don't let a capped `sc_adj` fully override a
  floored `hot_adj`).

Both are real, distinct, additive problems — not competing explanations. A v4.6 that only touches
the exponents would still leave the same 13 players cycling through the Bet tier; a v4.6 that only
addresses the staleness/persistence issue would still leave the underlying signal misweighted for
the fresh players who do rotate through. Scoping should treat these as two separate workstreams.
