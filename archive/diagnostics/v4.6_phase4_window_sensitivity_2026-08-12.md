Report only, per instruction. Phase 4 implementation held pending this decision. (Phase 3's

## Method

Same 8 biweekly checkpoints used for the original cap derivation (`archive/diagnostics/
v4.6_phase3_4_proposal_2026-08-12.md`). At each checkpoint, pulled league-wide HR/PA (all 30 teams,
`teams/stats?stats=byDateRange`) for three short-window lengths (7, 14, 21 days) ending on that
checkpoint date, each compared against the same 90-day long reference already computed.

## Direction: stable. Magnitude and cap-triggering: not stable.

**No directional disagreement anywhere** — at every one of the 8 checkpoints, all three window
lengths land on the same side of 1.0 (all above or all below). If the only question were "is the
league running hot or cold right now," any of the three windows would give the same answer.

**But magnitude diverges meaningfully, and it changes system behavior, not just the number.**
Per-checkpoint spread between the three window choices ranges from 0.014 (2026-08-11, essentially
no divergence) to **0.191** (2026-07-29) — on that date, the 7-day window reads a −20.9% swing
while the 21-day window on the *same date* reads only −1.9%. That's not a rounding-level
difference; it's the gap between "this would clip hard against the proposed cap" and "this is
barely worth mentioning."

**Cap-trigger check (±15%, i.e. outside [0.85, 1.15]) — 3 of 8 checkpoints (37.5%) disagree on
whether the cap would have fired at all, depending only on which window length was picked:**

|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-06-24 | ok (1.114) | **CAPPED** (1.190) | **CAPPED** (1.156) |
| 2026-07-01 | ok (1.099) | ok (1.102) | **CAPPED** (1.151) |

This meets the divergence bar as you defined it — not direction, but cap-triggering: whether
`league_adj` gets clipped (and therefore whether its full log-odds effect reaches every single
pick that day) depends on a choice between 7/14/21 days that has no independent justification
behind it, not just a magnitude difference that washes out in the noise.

**One clear pattern in the divergence, worth naming even though it doesn't resolve which window to
use:** the **7-day window is visibly the noisiest** — it produces both the single most extreme
reading in the whole dataset (−20.9% on 2026-07-29, well beyond what 14d or 21d ever show on any
checkpoint) and generally the largest checkpoint-to-checkpoint jumps. This is expected (smallest N
per checkpoint, ~8,000 PA vs ~22,000 at 21 days) and is evidence *against* 7 days specifically, but
doesn't by itself tell us whether 14 or 21 is the better-justified choice between the other two —
they agree at 6 of 8 checkpoints but still diverge enough to flip cap status at 2 of the 8.

## Holding per your instruction

This doesn't resolve to "14 days is robust to reasonable window choice" — it resolves to "14 vs 21
days disagree often enough to matter, and 7 days is clearly noisier than either." Not picking one
arbitrarily. Options, not resolved:

1. **Drop to a single, wider window** and skip the "short vs. long" ratio structure — e.g. widen
   the cap so it doesn't bind on ordinary 14-vs-21 disagreement, accepting less precision.
2. **Pick 21 days over 14** on the concrete evidence that 7 is noisier and 21 is the more stable of
   the remaining two (smallest per-checkpoint spread against 7-day in 7 of 8 comparisons) — this
   would be a data-supported choice, unlike the original consistency-with-`hot_adj` reasoning, but
   still means accepting whatever 21 gives you over 14 without independent confirmation that 21 is
   *right*, only that it's *more stable than 7*.
3. **Average or blend multiple window lengths** (e.g., mean of 14d and 21d ratios) to reduce
   single-window noise — a new mechanism, would need its own justification and impact check before
   proposing it as a real option rather than just naming it.
4. **Hold `league_adj` for this release** — Phase 4 was scoped as independent of Phases 1-3;
   nothing else in the release depends on it landing this checkpoint.

Not proceeding with implementation until you weigh in.
