**Does error scale with `hot_adj`/`sc_adj` magnitude even after v4.5 shrinkage? No — and this is

| Factor | corr(\|log-dev\|, calib error) |
|---|--:|
| `hot_adj` | +0.040 |
| `sc_adj` | +0.030 |
| `sp_adj` | +0.002 |

All four are effectively zero. Restricting to the `model_prob > 15%` population changes nothing
(`hot_adj` +0.015, `sc_adj` +0.008, `sp_adj` −0.008, `bp_adj` +0.003). **Overprediction is not
concentrated in the picks with the largest hot/Statcast adjustments — it's broadly and roughly
uniformly present across the whole high-probability population, regardless of adjustment size.**

missing for 1/611, `bp_adj` missing for 38/611 (6.2%) of the 15%+ population — present but not
large enough to be driving the aggregate result.

**Why this matters for the shrinkage hypothesis (ties into §7):** if small-sample noise in
`hot_adj`/`sc_adj` were the driver of overprediction, error should be worst where the raw sample was
smallest (largest shrinkage-relevant magnitude). It isn't. A second cut confirms this from a
different angle — splitting Bet-tier picks by whether `hot_adj`/`sc_adj` sit at their hard cap:

| | n | Pred | Actual | Diff |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `hot_adj` at cap (≥1.275) | 21 | 27.0% | 9.5% | −17.5pp |
| `hot_adj` not at cap | 37 | 28.6% | 5.4% | −23.2pp |
| `sc_adj` at cap (≥1.145) | 55 | 28.3% | 7.3% | −21.0pp |
| `sc_adj` not at cap | 3 | 23.4% | 0.0% | −23.4pp |

Not-at-cap picks are *as bad or worse* than at-cap picks, not better. Whatever is driving the
overprediction, it is not simply "the shrinkage caps let through a few extreme, noisy values" —
that story would predict at-cap picks to be the worst offenders, and they aren't.

**Separately, a striking population-level fact worth flagging on its own: 55 of 58 Bet-tier picks
(94.8%) have `sc_adj` sitting at or within 0.005 of its 1.15 cap.** (`hot_adj` is less extreme: 21/58,
36.2%, at its 1.28 cap.) Combined with the near-zero magnitude/error correlation above, this suggests
the v4.5 shrinkage constants (`HOT_SHRINKAGE_K`, `SC_SHRINKAGE_K`) are close to irrelevant *at the
Bet tier specifically* — almost every player who clears the Bet-tier bar already has enough BBE/PA
to push `sc_adj` to its ceiling regardless of shrinkage strength, so the binding constraint on
`sc_adj` for this population is the **cap value** (1.15), not the shrinkage weight. See §7.

## 7. Shrinkage parameter review

**Current constants (`mlb_hr_model.py`):** `HOT_SHRINKAGE_K = 40`, `SC_SHRINKAGE_K = 100`, continuous
form `w = n / (n + k)`, applied before the existing hard clips (`hot_adj` 0.78–1.28, `sc_adj`
0.78–1.15, unchanged from v4.4).

**Observed shrinkage weights on Bet-tier picks:** L14 PA (`r_pa`) ranges 9–61 (mean 48.5) → mean
`hot_adj` weight on raw rate ≈ 0.538 (range 0.184–0.604). Statcast BBE (`sc_bbe`) ranges 56–369
(mean 241.8) → mean `sc_adj` weight ≈ 0.677 (range 0.359–0.787). Both are well past their
low-sample floor for the players who actually reach Bet tier — these are established regulars with
real BBE/PA counts, not small-sample flukes.

**Does the §2/§4 evidence support pulling `k`/`k'` in further (more shrinkage)? No — the data argues
against it, for two independent reasons found this checkpoint:**

1. §4's near-zero correlation between adjustment magnitude and calibration error, and the finding
   that not-at-cap picks are *not* better calibrated than at-cap picks, means more shrinkage on the
   already-shrunk mid-range values wouldn't obviously help — the error isn't where the shrinkage
   would bite.
2. §4's 94.8% cap-saturation rate on `sc_adj` for Bet-tier picks means the shrinkage weight is
   frequently not the binding constraint at all for this population — the **cap** (1.15) is. Tuning
   `k'` (e.g. 100→150) would change `sc_adj` for borderline non-Bet-tier picks but leave the Bet-tier
   population's `sc_adj` values almost unchanged, since they're clipped regardless.
3. §2a's finding that cold-tagged (`hot_adj < 1`) picks are *more* overpredicted than hot-tagged
   ones is also inconsistent with "the shrinkage isn't aggressive enough" — if under-shrinkage were
   the problem, extreme positive `hot_adj` values should show the worst overprediction, and they
   don't.

**Read together, this checkpoint's evidence points away from the shrinkage-constant hypothesis and
toward two other candidate explanations that v4.5 didn't address:** (a) the cap values themselves
(1.15 / 1.28) may still be set too loose relative to what the shrunk-but-capped population actually
delivers — same lever v4.4 pulled once (1.35→1.15) without fixing the core problem; and (b) §6's
player-repetition finding, which points at a persistence/selection problem (the same handful of
players get re-flagged and keep losing) rather than a per-pick sample-size problem that empirical-
Bayes shrinkage is designed to fix. Neither is a proposed change here — flagging both as the more
promising lines of investigation than a further `k`/`k'` adjustment, based on what this checkpoint's
data actually shows.

## 8. League-wide HR environment / ASB step-change

**What baseline does the model actually use?** Confirmed from `mlb_hr_model.py`: `sr` (season rate)
comes from `stats=season&group=hitting&season={yr}` — MLB's live cumulative season-to-date total,
queried fresh on each run. It is **not** split or weighted by recency; a player's full season
(opening day through the pick date) is compressed into one rate. `picks.json` stores only the
derived `season_rate` (not raw season PA/HR), so this section's player-level split was computed via
live MLB Stats API calls (season totals + `byDateRange` from 2026-07-15 to today), not from stored
data — flagged as a data-availability gap for future diagnostics, same category as the v4.4
checkpoint's missing-`r_pa` issue.

**2026 All-Star Game: 2026-07-14** (confirmed via MLB Stats API `schedule?gameType=A&season=2026`).
Used 2026-07-15 as the post-ASB cutoff.

**League-wide HR/PA, pre- vs post-ASB (all 30 teams, live MLB Stats API):**

| Window | PA | HR | HR/PA |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Pre-ASB (2026-03-26 – 2026-07-13) | 109,227 | 3,359 | 3.075% |
| Post-ASB (2026-07-15 – 2026-08-11) | 26,013 | 763 | **2.933%** |

The league environment **cooled**, not heated, after the break — a −0.142pp absolute / −4.6%
relative decline. This is directionally consistent with (not opposite to) the overprediction
pattern found throughout this report: a season rate `sr` built mostly on pre-ASB PA is a mild
*overestimate* of a player's current true rate, in the same direction as the observed miscalibration
— but the magnitude is far too small to be a meaningful driver. A −4.6% relative shift on the
baseline rate translates to roughly a similar-order shift in per-PA probability before compounding,
nowhere near the −10 to −20pp gaps seen in the 20-99% bucket and the Bet tier.

**Per-player pre-ASB PA share, the 28 Bet-tier players (current cumulative stats as of 2026-08-11 —
note this is a same-day snapshot, not a reconstruction of what each historical pick date actually
saw; it will understate the pre-ASB share slightly for the earliest picks in this window, since
2026-08-03–08-10 games have since been added to the "post" bucket for all players alike):**

PA-weighted average pre-ASB share across these 28 players: **78.2%** (individual range 52.8%–90.7%).
This is high across the board — expected, since the ASB (2026-07-14) is only ~4 weeks before this
diagnostic window, so most of any player's season PA was necessarily accrued pre-break regardless of
which player you pick. There isn't much variation to exploit: this isn't "some players are stale and
some aren't," it's "everyone in this population is pre-ASB-heavy right now," which limits how much a
within-population correlation check can tell us.

**Correlation between pre-ASB share and calibration error, per Bet-tier pick:** +0.082 (essentially
none). Splitting at 60% share: picks on players with ≥60% pre-ASB share average −20.15pp calibration
error; the small <60%-share group (n=6, 2 players: Esmerlyn Valdez, Spencer Jones) averages **worse**,
−29.93pp — the opposite of what the pre-ASB-staleness hypothesis would predict. Given n=6 in the
low-share group this is not strong evidence against the hypothesis either, but it does not support it.

**`sc_adj`'s implicit reference point:** `LG_BARREL` (0.067) and `LG_HARD_HIT` (0.385) are hardcoded
constants in `mlb_hr_model.py`, not derived from any live-season query — they don't track pre/post-ASB
drift because they were never date-aware to begin with (unlike `sr`, which is always current). The
double-counting risk the task asked about is real but indirect: if a future fix adds a single global
"league HR environment" multiplier (e.g., something tracking the post-ASB −4.6% shift found above)
and *also* refreshes `LG_BARREL`/`LG_HARD_HIT` to a current-season value at the same time, both
changes could be capturing the same underlying macro driver (colder weather, deadened ball, etc.) if
that driver affects batted-ball quality and HR/PA together — worth checking explicitly, not doubling
up two corrections for one cause, if/when either change is designed. At present neither exists, so
there's no double-counting today.

**Bottom line on §8:** the mechanism is real and correctly directioned (post-ASB league HR rate is
lower, so an all-season baseline modestly overstates current form) but it is a minor contributor at
best — an order of magnitude too small to explain this checkpoint's overprediction, and the
within-population share/error correlation found no supporting signal. Not the primary lever for a
v4.6 fix; worth a small correction eventually, not worth chasing before the larger issues in §4/§6/§7.

## Overall assessment: should live staking pause?

**The data supports pausing.** Three consecutive checkpoints (v4.3, v4.4, v4.5) have now shown the
same core failure mode — overprediction concentrated in the highest-probability bucket, worsening
with edge magnitude, with Bet-tier ROI never once profitable. v4.5's shrinkage-based fix, specifically
designed to address the top two contributing factors identified in the v4.4 diagnostic, did not
resolve it: ROI (−70.97%) is worse than v4.4's already-bad −54.88%, though on a smaller resolved
sample (58 vs 96) so some of that gap is noise. More importantly, this checkpoint found the *reason*
the fix didn't work: the overprediction is not concentrated in the small-sample, high-magnitude
picks that empirical-Bayes shrinkage targets (§4's near-zero correlation), and 94.8% of Bet-tier
`sc_adj` values sit at their hard cap regardless of shrinkage strength (§4/§7) — meaning the next fix
needs to target the cap values and/or the player-repetition/persistence pattern found in §6, not the
shrinkage constants again. Continuing to stake real capital while that redesign happens risks
repeating the same −50-70% ROI pattern a fourth time.

## Open questions for Claude

1. **§8's per-player pre/post-ASB split used today's (2026-08-11) cumulative stats, not a
   reconstruction of what each historical pick date's `season` query actually returned.** This
   slightly understates pre-ASB share for the earliest picks in the window (2026-08-03) since 8 more
   days of post-ASB games have been folded in since. The direction of the finding (weak/no
   correlation) is unlikely to flip, but the exact percentages would shift a few points earlier in
   the window if reconstructed precisely — `picks.json` would need to persist raw season PA/HR
