## Step 1 — Collapse to a single combined term

**One correction to the framing before proposing a method:** barrel% and hard-hit% are not
collinear *with each other* — `corr(barrel_pct, hard_hit_pct) = 0.046` in the v4.5 sample, about as
uncorrelated as two real hitting metrics get. What's actually true, from Phase 1's offset-controlled
work, is that **each is separately correlated with the model's *other* factors** (`corr(offset,
barrel)=0.385`, `corr(offset, hard_hit)=0.547`) — i.e., confounding/mediation through "everything
else the model already knows about this player," not mutual collinearity. The practical conclusion
is the same either way — this data can't reliably separate two independent marginal exponents — so
collapsing to one term is still the right call, just for a slightly different statistical reason
than "collinear with each other."

**Proposed method: geometric mean of the two ratios, equal weight.**

```
barrel_ratio   = barrel_pct   / LG_BARREL
hh_ratio       = hard_hit_pct / LG_HARD_HIT
combined_ratio = sqrt(barrel_ratio * hh_ratio)
```

Equivalently, in the log-odds space the rest of the model already operates in:
`log(combined_ratio) = 0.5·log(barrel_ratio) + 0.5·log(hh_ratio)` — a straight 50/50 average of the
two log-ratios, replacing the old 0.40/0.20 split with an even split.

**Why equal weight, not something else:** with no reliable evidence favoring one input over the
other (every attempt to separate them either failed to reach significance or flipped sign across
cuts), equal weighting is the position that isn't quietly encoding an unsupported preference. I
considered a PCA-based combination as an alternative (letting the data determine the loadings rather
than assuming 50/50) and ran it as a check: first principal component of the two standardized
log-ratios loads **[0.7071, 0.7071]** — i.e., PCA independently arrives at the same equal weighting,
which is the expected result when two inputs have near-zero mutual correlation (uncorrelated inputs
→ PC1 loads equally on both by construction). Given that, PCA would add machinery (stored loadings,
a standardization step, a training/serving split) without changing the output — rejected in favor of
the simpler, fully-transparent geometric mean, which produces the identical result here.

## Step 2 — Cap (and why it can't be decided without also fixing the overall exponent)

**Correction (2026-08-12): the first version of this section was derived backwards, and I want the
record to show that rather than just quietly fixing it.** I swept `SC_GAMMA` and cap together and
picked the combination (0.20 / 1.08) because its resulting Bet-tier pin-rate (27.6%) "looked like a
reasonable reduction" from 94.8% — not too tight, not too loose. The park-factor comparison
(0.077 vs. park's 0.072 mean contribution) was computed *afterward*, as a sanity check on a number
already chosen. That's exactly the circular pattern flagged: the pin-rate reduction was the design
target, and the park-factor comparison was a post-hoc confirmation that happened to look tidy. Below
is the same section re-derived with the anchor decided first and the pin-rate reported only as an
observation.

**The structural point still stands and isn't affected by the correction:** Step 1's combined term,
taken at face value (`combined_ratio` with no further dampening), is *more* sensitive than the old
formula — the old exponents summed to 0.40+0.20 = **0.60** total; a bare geometric mean is exponent
0.5+0.5 = **1.0** total. So Step 2 still needs both a cap *and* an overall dampening exponent
(`SC_GAMMA`) applied to `combined_ratio` before the cap — the cap alone isn't a well-posed lever on
its own:

```
sc_adj_raw = combined_ratio ^ SC_GAMMA
sc_adj     = clip(sc_adj_raw, floor, cap)
```

**Cap — re-derived from the park-factor anchor, decided first this time.** `ln(cap)` directly bounds
`sc_adj`'s worst-case log-odds contribution *regardless of `SC_GAMMA`* (the clip always wins at the
boundary), so the park-factor anchor pins the cap independent of whatever `SC_GAMMA` turns out to be
— this piece of the original reasoning wasn't circular, it's the `SC_GAMMA` choice that was.
Anchor: `park_adj`'s mean `|log-odds|` contribution in the `model_prob>15%` population, **0.072**
(from the v4.5 checkpoint's factor-attribution table — an existing, independently-established
figure, not derived from this Statcast exercise).

```
cap = exp(0.072) = 1.0747  →  1.07
floor = 1/cap = 0.9305     →  0.93
```

**`SC_GAMMA` — this is the piece the park anchor doesn't pin down, and I don't have an equally clean
independent anchor for it.** The park-factor comparison only bounds the *ceiling*; it says nothing
about how strongly typical (non-extreme) values should move. Rather than sweep `SC_GAMMA` against
pin-rate outcomes again, I picked it via a fixed rule decided before looking at any resulting
distribution: **halve the old total exponent, 0.60 → 0.30.** This is still a judgment call, and I
want to be explicit that "halving" is itself somewhat arbitrary — it's not derived from the park
anchor, from Phase 1's data, or from anything except "a round, substantial, symmetric-feeling
discount." I don't think this is circular in the specific sense you flagged (it wasn't chosen by
looking at where the pin-rate landed), but it's also not as well-grounded as the cap. Flagging this
gap plainly rather than dressing it up as more principled than it is.

**Reproduction check (unchanged, still holds):** running the *old* formula (0.40/0.20 exponents,
k=100 shrinkage, cap 1.145) through this pipeline on the 58 Bet-tier picks reproduces **94.8%** at
cap exactly, matching the root-cause diagnostic.

**Resulting pin-rate under cap=1.07 / floor=0.93 / `SC_GAMMA`=0.30 — reported as an observation now,
not as the thing that was optimized for:**

| | ALL tracked (n=1,718) | Bet-tier (n=58) |
|---|--:|--:|
| At/above cap 1.07 | 26.5% | **86.2%** |
| At/below floor 0.93 | 13.9% | 0.0% |

**This is a materially worse-looking result than the original (circular) 27.6% figure** —
Bet-tier binding only drops from 94.8% to 86.2%, not to 27.6%. That's the honest cost of removing
the circularity: `SC_GAMMA`=0.30 is less aggressive than the pin-rate-optimized 0.20 was, so the
pre-clip distribution stays wider and more of it still reaches the (now lower) 1.07 cap. The cap
value itself is now properly anchored; the pin-rate outcome is what it is given that anchor and the
separately-chosen `SC_GAMMA` — I'm not adjusting either to make 86.2% look better, per your
instruction to report wherever it lands.

ground. `SC_GAMMA` is not — "halve it" is a plain judgment call with no anchor behind it, and the
pin-rate it produces (86.2% Bet-tier) is much closer to the old problem than the previous
(improperly-derived) proposal suggested. Three ways to go from here, not picking for you:
1. **Accept 0.30/1.07/0.93 as-is** — properly anchored on the cap, honest about `SC_GAMMA` being a
   round judgment call, and accept that Bet-tier picks will still often sit near the cap (86.2%),

2. **Push `SC_GAMMA` lower via the same fixed-rule logic** (e.g., quarter the old total, 0.60→0.15,

3. **Look for an independent anchor for `SC_GAMMA` too** before proceeding, the way the park factor
