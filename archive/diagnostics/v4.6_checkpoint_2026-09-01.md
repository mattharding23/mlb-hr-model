## 4. SC_GAMMA diagnostic

**Note on field names:** picks have no `sc_info` key. The Statcast inputs are in
`factors`: `barrel_pct`, `hard_hit_pct`, `sc_bbe`, `sc_adj`. `combined_ratio`
(pre-`SC_GAMMA`) was reconstructed from the model's own formula:
`sw = n/(n+100)`; `b_s = barrel·sw + 0.067·(1−sw)`; `hh_s = hard_hit·sw + 0.385·(1−sw)`;
`combined_ratio = sqrt((b_s/0.067)·(hh_s/0.385))`; check: `combined_ratio^0.30` clamped to
[0.93, 1.07] reproduces the stored `sc_adj` to max |Δ| = 8e-5.

Pool: 3,835 resolved picks with usable Statcast (`sc_bbe` present, `barrel_pct > 0`).
`sc_adj` pinned at the cap/floor for **5.5%** of them (vs 94.8% pinned under the old 1.15
cap in the v4.5 root-cause report — the re-anchored cap is doing what it was meant to).
`combined_ratio` distribution: min 0.76 · p25 0.96 · median 1.03 · p75 1.11 · max 1.52.

### 4a. OLS partial association

`(actual − model_prob) ~ 1 + log(combined_ratio) + log(split_adj) + log(hot_adj) +
log(sp_adj) + log(bp_adj) + log(park_adj)`, n = 3,835, **R² = 0.003**.

| Term | Coef | SE | t | p |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| const | −0.0199 | 0.0076 | −2.61 | 0.009 |
| **log(combined_ratio)** | **−0.1324** | 0.0506 | **−2.62** | **0.009** |
| log(split_adj) | −0.0522 | 0.0357 | −1.46 | 0.14 |
| log(hot_adj) | +0.0339 | 0.0262 | +1.29 | 0.20 |
| log(sp_adj) | −0.0119 | 0.0376 | −0.32 | 0.75 |
| log(bp_adj) | −0.0567 | 0.1001 | −0.57 | 0.57 |
| log(park_adj) | −0.0890 | 0.0574 | −1.55 | 0.12 |

Univariate `err ~ log(combined_ratio)`: coef −0.113, SE 0.048, t −2.33, p 0.020.

The partial association between contact quality and prediction error is **negative and
nominally significant** — net of the other factors, picks with *higher* reconstructed
contact quality are *more* overpredicted, i.e. the data leans toward the current
`SC_GAMMA = 0.30` being **too high, not too low** (a fit here would push it toward zero
or negative).

### 4b. Logistic implied-gamma fit

Reconstructed a game probability with the Statcast term removed
(`gp_noSC`: invert `model_prob` to per-PA log-odds, subtract `log(sc_adj)`, re-inflate by
`proj_pa`), then fit `outcome ~ 1 + logit(gp_noSC) + log(combined_ratio)` on the
non-`per_pa_capped` subset (n = 3,759):

| Term | Coef | SE | p |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| intercept | −1.245 | 0.212 | — |
| logit(gp_noSC) | +0.445 | 0.102 | — |
| **log(combined_ratio)** | **+0.893** | 0.554 | **0.107** |

This spec gives the **opposite sign** (positive) and is **not significant**. Its point
estimate would imply a total `SC_GAMMA` near ~0.9–1.2, but the SE spans zero to well past
2, and the `logit(gp_noSC)` slope of 0.44 (not ~1) shows the reconstruction is entangled
with the same overdispersion from §3, so the magnitude is not trustworthy.

### 4c. Conclusion

**Still inconclusive, and now sign-unstable across specification** — exactly the pattern
the v4.6 pre-launch refit found (`SC_GAMMA` is an explicit unanchored placeholder). The
OLS partial-association evidence leans mildly *negative* (against a positive Statcast
boost); the logistic offset spec leans positive but is non-significant with a huge SE.
R² ≈ 0 either way. **At n ≈ 3,800 the marginal Statcast signal is not distinguishable
from zero in a stable way.** Not strong enough to act on; the one directional hint (OLS
negative) is worth noting because it points the same way the v4.6 refit did.

## 5. SC_SHRINKAGE_K check

`sc_bbe` (L14 batted-ball events) on resolved picks, n = 4,912 with the field populated:

| min | p25 | median | p75 | max | mean |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 3 | 21 | **29** | 36 | 57 | 27.9 |

The distribution is tight (IQR 21–36) and small — consistent with the code comment that
`SC_SHRINKAGE_K = 100` was calibrated against **season-cumulative** BBE (~200 for a
half-season regular) and never revised after v4.6 switched `sc_adj` to a **14-day rolling
window** (~20–40 BBE for a regular).

**Implied shrinkage weight `w = n/(n+k)`:**

| | 25th (21) | median (29) | 75th (36) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| current **k = 100** | 0.17 | **0.225** | 0.26 |
| k = median = **29** | 0.42 | **0.50** | 0.55 |
| k = 40 | 0.34 | 0.42 | 0.47 |
| k = 25 | 0.46 | 0.54 | 0.59 |

**The math for an anchored k:** setting `w = n/(n+k) = 0.5` at the median implies
`k = median BBE = 29`. If instead the anchor is "the median regular's own L14 contact
quality should carry ≥50% weight," any `k ≤ 29` achieves that; `k` in the 25–40 range
keeps the IQR spread of weights roughly within 0.34–0.59.

**Read:** under the current `k = 100`, the median tracked player's own rolling-window
Statcast gets only **22.5% weight** — the term is ~78% league average for a typical
pick, which is most of why `sc_adj` sits near 1.0 for so many picks (§4: only 5.5%
even reach the cap/floor). This is a **structural mismatch between the constant and the
window it now feeds**, clearly documented by the data. Whether the right anchor is
"median → 50% weight" (k≈29) or something more conservative is a judgment call, not
something the distribution alone settles. Strength: the *mismatch* is a strong,
unambiguous finding; the *target weight* is a design choice.

## 6. League-wide HR rate / ASB step-change

**Caveat first:** the tracked sample is a **model-selected candidate pool** (every batter
with a posted line the model scored, from 0–5% projections up to 30%+), not a random PA
sample. Absolute HR/PA here is **not** the league rate. Only the *within-window trend*
and *relative* over/under are interpretable from this data.

**Tracked-sample HR rate, by ISO week (all resolved picks, not just Bet-tier):**

| Week | Dates | Picks | Σ PA | HR | HR/PA | HR/pick |
|--:|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 33 | 08-12→08-16 | 1,448 | 5,591 | 168 | **0.0300** | 0.116 |
| 34 | 08-17→08-23 | 1,969 | 7,687 | 191 | **0.0248** | 0.097 |
| 35 | 08-24→08-30 | 1,415 | 5,417 | 143 | **0.0264** | 0.101 |
| 36 | 08-31 only | 158 | 634 | 17 | 0.0268 | 0.108 |

First half of window (< 08-21): HR/PA 0.0272 · second half (≥ 08-21): 0.0265.

**Whole v4.6 sample, model vs actual:**
- Σ `model_prob` = 599.3 vs 519 actual HR → actual/predicted = **0.866** (13.4% aggregate
  overprediction across *all* tracked picks).
- Mean model per-PA implied rate 0.0298 vs actual tracked HR/PA 0.0269 → ratio **0.90**.

**Read on the post-ASB decline hypothesis (noted 2026-08-07 / examined in the v4.5
root-cause report, which measured league HR/PA 3.075% pre-ASB → 2.933% post-ASB,
**−4.6%**):**
- The tracked sample shows a drop from week 33 (0.0300) to weeks 34–36 (~0.025–0.027),
  but week 33 is only 5 dates and the later weeks are roughly flat — this is **weak,
  noisy support** for a lower current environment, not a clean step. The half-to-half
  move (0.0272 → 0.0265) is inside the noise.
- The sample-wide HR/PA (~0.027) is itself *below* the v4.5-measured post-ASB league rate
  (0.0293). That is a **selection artifact** (the pool is diluted with low-projection
  candidates) and cannot be read as "the league is now at 0.027."

**Rough sensitivity — how much of the calibration gap a corrected league baseline could
explain (holding everything else constant):**
- Aggregate overprediction is 13.4% (ratio 0.866); the 20–99% bucket needs a 0.79×
  multiplicative shrink (21% over); 15%+ combined needs 0.78×.
- `season_rate` (`sr`) is the channel that would carry a stale-league effect, and per-PA
  rate scales roughly linearly with `sr` near these values. If `sr` is ~4.6% too high
  (full post-ASB league decline) → removes ~4.6 of the 13.4 aggregate points
  (**~1/3 of the overall gap**) and ~4.6 of the ~21 high-bucket points
  (**~1/5 of the high-bucket gap**).
- More realistically, v4.5 found the tracked players average ~78% pre-ASB PA, so `sr`
  staleness is closer to 0.78 × 4.6% ≈ **3.6%** → ~1/4 of the overall gap, ~1/6 of the
  high-bucket gap.
- **The majority of the high-end overprediction is not a league-baseline problem** — it
  is the overdispersion/stacking pattern (consistent with the §3 slope of 0.63 and the
  §1 shape). A baseline correction would help modestly and mostly at the low/middle
  buckets; it would not by itself fix the 20%+ tier.

Strength: the *arithmetic bound* (baseline explains at most ~1/3, likely ~1/4) is solid;
the *existence and size* of a current league decline is only weakly evidenced by this
tracked sample and would need a direct MLB Stats API pull (as v4.5 did) to pin down.
