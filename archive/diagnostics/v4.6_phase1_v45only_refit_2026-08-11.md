# v4.6 Phase 1 Follow-Up — v4.5-Only Offset-Controlled Statcast Fit — 2026-08-11

Follow-up to `archive/diagnostics/v4.6_phase1_statcast_refit_2026-08-11.md` ("Fit 2"). Report only —
no code changed, `results/picks.json` still not reset. Source: `archive/v4.5/picks_v4.5.json` only
(the frozen Phase 0 snapshot), not pooled with v4.2–v4.4 this time, to isolate whether Fit 2's
v4.3-vs-v4.5 sign instability was a real signal problem or an artifact of pooling across the
v4.3→v4.4 switch from multiplicative-probability to additive-log-odds compounding.

## Method (unchanged from Phase 1's Fit 2)

Same offset construction: for each resolved, latest-window v4.5 pick, `offset = logit(game_prob) −
log(sc_adj)`, recompounded through `1−(1−per_pa)^pp` — i.e., "what the model would have predicted
for this game had `sc_adj` been left at 1.0." Then fit `logit(P(HR)) = offset + a + b1·log(barrel_pct
/ LG_BARREL) + b2·log(hard_hit_pct / LG_HARD_HIT)`. `per_pa_capped` picks excluded (inversion isn't
valid when the per-PA rate was clipped rather than computed directly) — this is the same exclusion
rule as Phase 1, just applied to the v4.5-only slice.

## Sample

| | n |
|---|--:|
| v4.5 resolved, latest-window | 1,806 |
| Usable for offset fit (excl. 48 capped, 88 missing-field) | **1,670** |
| HR events (`result=True`) | **170** (10.18% win rate) |
| Date range | 2026-08-02 to 2026-08-10 |

**Sample size sufficiency — genuinely mixed, flagging explicitly as requested.** By the standard
events-per-variable heuristic (Peduzzi et al., ≥10 events per fitted parameter for stable logistic
MLE), 170 events over 3 parameters = ~57 events/parameter is comfortably above the usual bar — on
that measure alone this looks fine. But the heuristic doesn't capture what's actually happening
here: `offset` is fairly strongly correlated with both predictors (`corr(offset, barrel)=0.385`,
`corr(offset, hard_hit)=0.547`) — expected, since the offset encodes "everything else the model
already knows is good about this player/matchup" and good hitters score well on Statcast too, but it
means a meaningful share of each predictor's variance is not independent information relative to what
the offset already carries. That shrinks the *effective* information available to pin down `b1`/`b2`
well below what the raw event count implies. The bootstrap results below make this concrete: this
sample is thinner than the EPV rule of thumb alone would suggest.

## Results

**Joint fit:**

| Coefficient | Estimate | SE (asymptotic) | 95% CI (asymptotic) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| intercept | −0.139 | 0.086 | [−0.307, +0.030] |
| **b1 (barrel)** | **+0.036** | 0.164 | [−0.285, +0.358] |
| **b2 (hard-hit)** | **−0.942** | 0.439 | **[−1.802, −0.083]** |

**Univariate (one predictor at a time):**

| Predictor | Estimate | SE | 95% CI |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| barrel alone | −0.169 | 0.149 | [−0.461, +0.124] |
| hard-hit alone | −0.892 | 0.381 | [−1.638, −0.145] |

**Bootstrap (1,000 resamples, same joint specification) — run because the asymptotic CI on a
sample this size, with this much offset/predictor correlation, is exactly the situation where the
normal approximation can understate true uncertainty:**

| Coefficient | Bootstrap mean | Bootstrap 95% (2.5–97.5 pct) |
|---|--:|--:|
| b1 (barrel) | +0.003 | [−0.465, +0.364] |
| b2 (hard-hit) | −0.841 | **[−1.747, +0.436]** |

**This is the key result.** The asymptotic Wald CI on `b2` (hard-hit) excludes zero
(−1.802 to −0.083) and would normally be read as a significant negative effect. The bootstrap CI on
the same fit, same data, is nearly twice as wide and **includes zero** (−1.747 to +0.436). The
point estimate stays negative in both (−0.94 asymptotic, −0.84 bootstrap mean), but the "statistical
significance" read depends entirely on which uncertainty estimate you trust, and the bootstrap is
the more trustworthy one at this sample size given the offset/predictor correlation noted above —
**the negative hard-hit coefficient should not be treated as a confidently-established effect.**

**Within-v4.5 chronological split (first half of the window vs. second half, as an additional
stability check independent of the pooling question):**

| | n | events | b1 (barrel) | b2 (hard-hit) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2026-08-02 – 08-05 | 821 | 87 | −0.082 ± 0.248 | −1.047 ± 0.673 |
| 2026-08-07 – 08-10 | 849 | 83 | +0.167 ± 0.223 | −0.784 ± 0.580 |

`barrel`'s point estimate flips sign between halves (both consistent with zero given the SEs —
not a real signal either half). `hard-hit`'s point estimate stays negative in both halves
(−1.05 and −0.78) — directionally consistent within v4.5's own window, which the pooled fit's
v4.3-vs-v4.5 comparison did not show.

## Comparison against pooled Fit 2

| | Pooled (v4.2–v4.5, n=9,243) | v4.5-only (n=1,670) |
|---|--:|--:|
| b1 (barrel) | −0.193 [−0.358, −0.029] (sig. negative) | +0.036 [−0.285, +0.358] (not sig.) |
| b2 (hard-hit) | +0.108 [−0.345, +0.561] (not sig.) | −0.942 [−1.802, −0.083] asymptotic / [−1.747, +0.436] bootstrap (not sig. under bootstrap) |

**Neither coefficient replicates the pooled fit's specific point estimate or significance pattern.**
Pooled had barrel significantly negative and hard-hit null; v4.5-only has barrel null and hard-hit
negative (significant under the asymptotic CI, not under bootstrap). Taken at face value this looks
like it swapped which coefficient is "the problem," which on its own would suggest real instability
rather than a clean resolution.

**But look at what's consistent, not just what point-estimate is largest:** across pooled Fit 2, all
four per-version fits from Phase 1, and both v4.5-only specifications here (joint, univariate,
both half-splits) — **no specification, in any version or sub-window, produces a robust, statistically
confident *positive* marginal coefficient for either barrel% or hard-hit% once the offset is
controlled.** Every point estimate across every cut is either near zero or negative; nothing comes
back cleanly positive and significant. That's the one finding that has held up through every version
of this fit, pooled or not, bootstrapped or not, split or not.

## Which outcome is this — (a) or (b)?

**Neither cleanly. It's closer to (b) than (a), but with one qualitative finding that does survive.**

- It is **not** outcome (a) as specified — the v4.5-only fit does not reproduce pooled Fit 2's
  specific direction/magnitude on either coefficient, and the bootstrap shows the v4.5-only sample
  can't even confidently establish its own headline "hard-hit negative" result as distinct from zero.
  Pooling was not simply adding noise to an otherwise-clean v4.5 signal — the v4.5-only sample has
  its own, separate instability (visible in the chronological split and the bootstrap gap).
- It is **largely outcome (b)** — this sample (170 events) is too thin to pin down a specific,
  trustworthy magnitude or confidently sign either coefficient on its own. The asymptotic
  significance on hard-hit doesn't survive a more appropriate uncertainty estimate at this size.
- **What does survive, and is worth weighting in the decision:** the complete absence of any
  positive, significant marginal Statcast signal anywhere I've cut this data — pooled, per-version,
  v4.5-only, univariate, bootstrapped, or split by date. That's a consistent, repeated null result
  across roughly a dozen different specifications, which is itself informative even though no
  specification pins down a precise negative magnitude to act on.

## Tradeoff for Phase 2 scoping — laid out, not resolved

**Option A — proceed with Fit 1's magnitudes (barrel ≈0.15, hard-hit ≈1.15) as a conservative
placeholder, revisit at a future checkpoint with more v4.5 data.**
- Pro: Fit 1 is the one stable, well-powered result across this whole exercise (n=9,791 pooled,
  consistent sign and rough magnitude across all four per-version fits). Gives Phase 2b/2c/Phase 3
  concrete numbers to build against instead of stalling the release.
- Con: per Phase 1's original flag, Fit 1's coefficients don't net out what the model's other
  factors already explain — baking them in risks the double-counting this whole investigation
  exists to avoid. Every offset-controlled cut done since (pooled and v4.5-only) points away from
  Fit 1's specific magnitudes being the right ones to add on top of the rest of the model.

**Option B — leave `sc_adj`'s weighting/exponents untouched this release; scope v4.6 to Phases 2b
(rolling window), 2c (cap re-derivation on whatever exponents remain), 3 (interaction rule), and 4
(league_adj) only.**
- Pro: doesn't act on a coefficient estimate that this diagnostic itself can't confidently sign,
  let alone size. The one thing that *has* replicated consistently — no evidence of positive
  marginal lift — is itself weak grounds for a specific reweighting, but reasonably strong grounds
  for "don't increase trust in this factor's current form." Keeping `HOT_SHRINKAGE_K`/`SC_SHRINKAGE_K`-
  style formula shape but leaving 0.40/0.20 as-is doesn't add new unvalidated behavior in a release
  that already has three other substantial changes.
- Con: 0.40/0.20 were never validated in the first place (Phase 1's original finding) — "leave them
  alone" isn't neutral, it's continuing to ship two numbers with no empirical support behind them
  either, just the familiar ones. Also leaves the root-cause diagnostic's original §2/§4 finding
  (barrel% not translating to actual outcome lift within the high-probability zone) unaddressed.

**A third possibility, not in your original framing but worth naming given what actually came back:**
since no specification produced a reliable *positive* marginal coefficient anywhere, a version of
Option B that also **shrinks `sc_adj`'s overall log-odds contribution toward a smaller cap** (Phase
2c territory, re-derived the same outcome-based way Phase 1 attempted for the exponents) without
touching the 0.40/0.20 split between barrel and hard-hit specifically, would be consistent with what
actually replicated (weak-to-no marginal value) without requiring a precise, individually-fitted
exponent pair that this data can't currently support.

Not picking between these — flagging the tradeoff as asked, holding at this checkpoint.
