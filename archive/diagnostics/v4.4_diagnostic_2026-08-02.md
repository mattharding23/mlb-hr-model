## 4. Factor-level attribution, model_prob > 15%

n = 1,154 resolved picks with `model_prob > 0.15`. Raw win rate 14.7% (170-984) vs. average
predicted 21.6% — an 6.9pp overprediction gap, consistent with §2.

**Mean log-odds contribution per factor** (higher = pushes probability up more):

| Factor | Mean log-odds | Mean \|log-odds\| |
|---|--:|--:|
| `sc_adj` (Statcast) | +0.1123 | 0.1247 |
| `hot_adj` (L14 hotness) | +0.0468 | 0.1808 |
| `split_adj` (handedness) | +0.0272 | 0.0941 |
| `wind_adj` | +0.0058 | 0.0058 |
| `temp_adj` | +0.0032 | 0.0068 |
| `park_adj` | +0.0023 | 0.0614 |
| `pitch_blend` (55% SP / 45% BP) | −0.0247 | 0.0621 |

`sc_adj` has the largest mean *positive* push and is the single largest contributor to why
these picks clear 15%+ in the first place. `hot_adj` has a smaller mean push but by far the
largest *volatility* (mean |log-odds| 0.18, more than double any other factor) — it swings
picks both up and down more than anything else in the model, consistent with it being the
least-shrunk factor relative to its underlying sample sizes (see §7).

**Which factor dominates each individual pick** (largest single positive log-odds term):

| Dominant factor | n (of 1,154) | n among overpredicted-tail losers (of 984) |
|---|--:|--:|
| `hot_adj` | 492 | 419 (42.6% of losers) |
| `sc_adj` | 447 | 377 (38.3% of losers) |
| `split_adj` | 124 | 113 |
| `park_adj` | 69 | 57 |
| `pitch_blend` | 19 | 15 |
| `wind_adj` | 2 | 2 |
| `temp_adj` | 1 | 1 |

`hot_adj` and `sc_adj` together are the dominant factor on **81%** of all 15%+ picks and
**81%** of the overpredicted-tail losers — proportionally identical to their share of the
full population, so neither factor is *disproportionately* concentrated in the losing tail
by this measure alone. But `hot_adj` is the single most common dominant factor overall
(492/1,154, 42.6%), making it the largest lever on which picks reach the 15%+ zone at all.

**L14 sample-size check — data limitation, not a null result:** `results/picks.json` does
not persist the raw L14 plate-appearance count (`r_pa`) used inside `hot_adj`'s regression
weight — only the final derived ratio (`hot_adj`) is written to `factors`. The raw
components (`r_hr`, `r_pa`, and the season rate `sr` that `hot_adj` is computed relative to)
are not recoverable from stored data. This request cannot be answered directly from
`picks.json` as currently structured (flagged in Open Questions).

As an indirect proxy: of the 1,154 picks, 337 (29.2%) have `hot_adj ≥ 1.27` (at/near its
1.28 cap) and 216 (18.7%) have `hot_adj ≤ 0.79` (at/near its 0.78 floor) — 48% of all 15%+
picks are sitting at or within 1 point of a `hot_adj` bound. Per the regression-weight
formula in the live code (§7), the weight on the raw L14 rate saturates at its 0.45 cap once
`r_pa ≥ ~20.25` — roughly 5 games. That means a pick can hit `hot_adj`'s extreme bound off a
sample as small as ~20 PA, and getting more data beyond that point does *not* pull the
estimate back toward the mean any further. This is consistent with (but does not prove,
given the missing raw-PA data) the small-sample-swing pattern the task asked to check for.

## 5. Odds-cap gate: protective or not?

109 raw-edge≥5pp picks were gated by `max_odds` across the full window; 95 resolved; 77
survive the same latest-window dedup (`counts_toward_roi != False`) used for the Bet-tier
figures in §1, making the two sets directly comparable.

| Set | n | W-L | Hypothetical/actual net | Staked | ROI |
|---|--:|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **Gated (max_odds), hypothetical** | 77 | 9-68 (11.7%) | +0.35u | 1.82u | **+19.11%** |
| **Passed-gate Bet-tier, actual** | 96 | 9-87 (9.4%) | −1.17u | 2.14u | **−54.88%** |

Hypothetical staking uses the same quarter-Kelly stake already computed and stored for each
gated pick (`quarter_kelly`, capped 0.03u) and the actual listed `best_odds`, i.e. exactly
the sizing the pick would have received had it passed the gate.

Gated-set edge range 5.0–25.4pp (median 10.7pp), odds range +525 to +2000 (median +700). The
9 gated wins are spread across 5 different dates (2026-07-22 ×4, 07-24, 07-27 ×3, 08-01) with
individual profits of +0.09u to +0.45u — not a single lucky longshot carrying the whole
result.

**Finding: the gate is not doing useful protective filtering in this window — if anything it
points the wrong direction.** The picks it excludes would hypothetically have finished
*profitably* (+19.1% ROI) over the same period the picks it lets through lost over half
their staked capital (−54.9% ROI). This directly contradicts the v4.3 diagnostic's basis for
keeping `max_odds` as "carrying essentially all the gates' protective value" — that
conclusion was drawn from v4.3 data and has not held up in v4.4. That said, n=77 (9 wins) in
the gated set is a small sample; a positive-ROI read this early is consistent with, but not
proof against, high variance in the underlying longshot-odds win rate. The two populations
should be re-compared once the gated set has more resolved picks before treating +19% as a
stable estimate — but the current data gives no support for the gate being the thing
protecting the model from itself. The real underperformance is concentrated in the picks
that *do* clear the gate.

## 7. Shrinkage check (current code, as-is — no fix proposed)

**Hotness / L14 (`mlb_hr_model.py`, factor 2):**
```
w = min(r_pa / 45, 0.45)        # requires r_pa >= 5 to apply at all
hot_adj = clip(((r_hr/r_pa)*w + sr*(1-w)) / sr, 0.78, 1.28)
```
The regression weight on the raw (unshrunk) L14 rate reaches its **full 0.45 cap at
r_pa ≈ 20.25** — about 5 games at a typical ~4 PA/game. Beyond that point, additional L14
sample size does not increase shrinkage toward the season mean; the weight is flat. A batter
with 21 PA over 5 hot games gets the same maximum regression weight as one with 55 PA over
14 games.

**Statcast (`mlb_hr_model.py`, factor 4):**
```
sc_adj = clip((barrel/LG_BARREL)^0.40 * (hard_hit/LG_HARD_HIT)^0.20, 0.78, 1.15)
```
v4.4 only tightened the upper bound (1.35 → 1.15, log-odds cap ln(1.35)≈0.30 →
ln(1.15)≈0.14). The exponents (0.40 barrel, 0.20 hard-hit) are unchanged from earlier
versions, and — unlike `hot_adj` and `split_adj` — there is no PA/sample-size-based
regression weight applied to the underlying barrel%/hard-hit% rates at all; the exponents
are the *only* dampening mechanism, applied uniformly regardless of how many batted-ball
events the season rate is built on.

**Plain assessment given §4–§6:** these do not look adequate. `hot_adj` and `sc_adj` are the
two dominant factors behind both the general overprediction pattern (§2, §4) and the
concrete 08-01 loss cluster (§6). `hot_adj`'s weight saturates at a genuinely small PA
threshold (~20 PA) with no further shrinkage past that point, and `sc_adj` applies no
PA-based shrinkage at all — only a fixed exponent and a v4.4-tightened cap. The v4.4 cap
reduction (change #2 in `VERSION_HISTORY.md`) addressed the *ceiling* on `sc_adj`'s
contribution but not the underlying lack of sample-size-aware regression that let a
low-PA Statcast read reach that ceiling in the first place. This matches the diagnostic
evidence without yet proposing a specific parameter change.

## Open questions for Claude

1. **L14 raw PA is not recoverable from `picks.json`.** §4's request to check L14 sample
   size directly against probability swings could only be answered indirectly (via
   `hot_adj`'s distance from its bounds + the weight-formula's saturation point in the live
   code). If a genuine PA-level breakdown is needed, `check_results.py` or the pick-writer in
   `mlb_hr_model.py` would need to persist `r_pa`/`r_hr` (and the analogous split `s_pa`) into
   `factors` going forward — this diagnostic cannot retroactively reconstruct them for the
   3,267 already-resolved picks. Flagging for awareness only; no code changed here.
2. **§5's positive gated-set ROI (+19.1%, n=77, 9 wins) is a small, early sample.** The
   direction is clear and the wins aren't concentrated in one lucky pick, but I would not
   treat +19.1% as a stable estimate of what un-gating would actually produce at scale — it
   should be revisited once the gated population has meaningfully more resolved picks.
3. **§3's edge-monotonicity read is entangled with the odds gate by construction** (no
   passed-gate pick exists above ~14pp edge). I've reported both what's visible in the gated
   Bet-tier range (still negative, still worsening with edge) and the confound with §5's
   gated-set numbers, but a clean, un-confounded edge-vs-ROI curve across the *full* edge
   range would require either loosening `MAX_BET_ODDS` in a controlled test or tracking
   diagnostic-only ROI on gated picks for longer — I'm not proposing either, just noting the
   read in §3 is necessarily partial.
4. **§6's "two-game concentration" finding is descriptive, not causal.** I did not
   investigate why WSH@ATL (824890) and the CIN game (824485) specifically produced 8 losing
   picks between them (e.g., whether it was a park/weather/pitching outlier for that day) —
   that's outside this diagnostic's scope as specified, but would be the natural next
   question if this pattern recurs.
